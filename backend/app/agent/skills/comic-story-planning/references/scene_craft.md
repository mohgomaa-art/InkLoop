# Comic Scene Craft & Micro-Dynamics

A comic scene is a unit of dramatic or emotional progression. Every scene must leave the story in a different state from where it began.

---

## 1. The Proactive Scene Cycle (Goal -> Conflict -> Disaster)

Applied to fast-paced, plot-advancing scenes:

1. **Goal**: The character enters the scene wanting something concrete, immediate, and visible (e.g., retrieving a stolen key, convincing an ally to stay, fixing a broken engine before dusk).
2. **Conflict**: Opposition arises immediately. The obstacle can be:
   - *Physical / Environmental*: A locked gate, sudden blizzard, falling rubble.
   - *Interpersonal*: An ally hesitates, a bystander interrupts, an adversary counters.
   - *Internal*: Reluctance to hurt someone, fear of heights, hesitation to reveal a secret.
3. **Disaster / Complication**: The scene does NOT end with simple success. It ends with:
   - *Yes, but...*: They get the key, but the alarm triggers.
   - *No, and furthermore...*: They fail to unlock the door, and the guards corner them.

---

## 2. The Reactive Scene Cycle (Reaction -> Dilemma -> Decision)

Applied to emotional, contemplative, or dramatic consequence scenes:

1. **Reaction**: The visceral emotional aftermath of the preceding disaster (shock, grief, rage, exhausted laughter). Often best told with silent visual panels or subtle body language.
2. **Dilemma**: The character faces a choice between two imperfect options (risk the climb alone vs abandon the mission; tell the painful truth vs keep the lie).
3. **Decision**: The character commits to a new proactive course of action, which immediately provides the Goal for the next proactive scene.

---

## 3. Engineering the Scene Turning Point

Every effective scene contains a pivot where emotional or informational polarity flips:
- *Positive to Negative*: Hopeful investigation reveals a catastrophic betrayal.
- *Negative to Positive*: Desperate defeat reveals an unexpected ally or hidden resource.
- *Curiosity to Dread*: An innocent puzzle turns out to be an active trap.

### Diagnostic Checklist for Every Planned Scene
- [ ] Does the scene change the narrative state? (If removed, does the plot still make sense? If yes, cut or consolidate it.)
- [ ] Is the protagonist making tactical choices or merely observing?
- [ ] Is the dialogue essential, or could the beat be told more powerfully through visual acting?
- [ ] Does the final panel launch the reader forward into the next scene?
