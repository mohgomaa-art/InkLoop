# Comics Adaptation & Visual Storytelling

Adapting storytelling to comics requires thinking in images, rhythm, reading flow, and page geography.

---

## 1. Visual Translation: Show vs Imply

In comics, every square millimeter is composed. Choose the most dynamic visual fraction of an action:

| Story Event | Weak / Prose Translation | Strong Visual Comic Adaptation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Character realizes they are trapped | Character says: "We are trapped, the door won't budge!" | Wide shot: small character silhouette pulling desperately at an iron vault door beneath a looming, impassable shadow. |
| Time passing in contemplation | Caption: "Three hours passed as he thought." | Aspect-to-aspect or silent sequence: melting candle wick, tea steam fading, coffee cup empty next to crumpled plans. |
| Tension before an explosion | Characters arguing loudly | Extreme close-up on a trembling second hand, sweat dropping on a wire, eyes narrowing. |

---

## 2. Panel Transition Taxonomy (McCloud Framework)

Match the transition type to the intended reading velocity:

1. **Moment-to-Moment**: Requires very little closure. Slows down time for maximum suspense, intimate emotional acting, or micro-reactions (e.g. eye closing, drop falling).
2. **Action-to-Action**: The standard engine of comic storytelling. Shows a single subject moving from preparation to execution to outcome.
3. **Subject-to-Subject**: Stays within a single scene or idea, moving between different participants or elements (e.g., speaker -> listener's clenching hand -> ticking clock).
4. **Scene-to-Scene**: Transports reader across significant gaps of space or time (e.g., "Meanwhile, across the city...").
5. **Aspect-to-Aspect**: Wandering eye transitions common in Japanese manga. Establishes mood, atmosphere, season, or psychological stillness by showing fragments of an environment (clouds, lanterns, fallen leaves).

---

## 3. Page Density & Emotional Rhythm

Panel density dictates perceived reading speed and emotional scale:

- **1 Panel (Splash Page)**: Maximum emotional impact, monumental reveal, breathtaking vista, or story climax. Demands awe and stillness.
- **2 to 3 Panels**: Majestic, deliberate, or deep conversation. Allows panoramic horizons or full-figure character staging.
- **4 to 5 Panels**: Standard comic pacing. Balanced rhythm of establishing, action, reaction, and dialogue.
- **6 to 8 Panels**: Rapid-fire dialogue, frantic combat, kinetic chase, or claustrophobic interrogation. High reading velocity.

---

## 4. Reading Direction & Page Geography

- **Western (LTR)**: Left-to-right, top-to-bottom. Reader enters top-left, exits bottom-right.
- **Manga (RTL)**: Right-to-left, top-to-bottom. Reader enters top-right, exits bottom-left.
- **The Page-Turn Reveal**:
  - In paged print or double-spread readers, the bottom-outside corner is the last panel read before flipping the physical/virtual page.
  - Never put the climax or reveal at the top of an odd page where the eye can spoil it prematurely.
  - Put the trigger/question at the bottom-outside panel, and place the dramatic payoff as the lead panel of the turned page.
